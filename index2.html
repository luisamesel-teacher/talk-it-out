START HERE

have, has, haven't, hasn't

FORM
have / has + past participle
WHO GETS WHICH

I · you · we · they → have
he · she · it → has

The helper is never optional. Without have or has, there is no present perfect — only a verb standing alone.
EXAMPLES
  • Supermarkets have wasted tons of food.Affirmative: helper + participle.
  • She hasn't finished her homework.Negative: not comes after the helper.
  • Have you seen that campaign?Question: the helper jumps to the front.

FIX THE SENTENCE
My parents has seen that campaign before. Tap to fix
My parents has have seen that campaign before. My parents = they. Two people never take has.
She not has arrived yet. Tap to fix
She not has hasn't arrived yet. In English not comes after the helper, never before it. This is the Portuguese order sneaking in.
THE SECOND PIECE

The past participle

THE RULE

Regular verbs: verb + -ed, the same as the past.
Irregular verbs: a third form you have to know.

The past and the participle are not always the same word. Went is a past. Gone is a participle. Only the participle can follow have or has.
CHOOSE ONE

She has _____ her lunch already.

Aate
Beaten
Tap to check
AAte is the past. It goes alone: She ate her lunch at noon.
Beat · ate · eaten. After has, use the third form.
CHOOSE ONE

They have _____ a big mistake.

Amade
Bmaked
Tap to check
Amake · made · made. Here the past and the participle are the same word.
BIrregular verbs never take -ed. There is no maked.
WATCH OUT
I have go to the supermarket. → I have gone to the supermarket.
We have buy the vegetables. → We have bought the vegetables.
He has write three books. → He has written three books.
THE BIG QUESTION

Which one do I use?

PAST SIMPLE
I threw it away yesterday.The time is known: yesterday, last week, in 2023, two days ago.
PRESENT PERFECT
I have already thrown it away.The time is not said, or the period hasn't finished: today, this week, this year.
THE GIVEAWAY WORDS

yesterday · last week · in 2023 · two days ago → past simple
ever · never · just · already · yet · this week → present perfect
