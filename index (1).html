THE BASELINE

Past simple

FORM
subject + verb-ed / irregular verb
EXAMPLES
  • I got home at eleven.It happened and it's over.
  • She trained every day last year.She doesn't do it anymore.
Every tense below is the past simple plus one extra idea. Compare them with this one.
IN PROGRESS

Past continuous

FORM
was / were + verb-ing
THE EXTRA IDEA

The action wasn't finished. It was happening at that moment in the past.

EXAMPLES
  • It was raining when I arrived.The rain started before I arrived and kept going.
  • I was studying at eight last night.I was in the middle of it at that time.
SIMPLE OR CONTINUOUS?
SIMPLE
When she called, I cooked dinner.First the call, then I cooked.
CONTINUOUS
When she called, I was cooking dinner.I was already in the middle of cooking.
CHOOSE ONE

I _____ my homework when the lights went out.

Awas doing
Bdid
Tap to check
✓A — was doing. Yes. I was in the middle of the homework when the lights went out.
✕B — did. This means I did the homework after the lights went out. You need the action that was already happening.
PAST TOUCHING NOW

Present perfect

FORM
have / has + past participle
THE EXTRA IDEA

It happened in the past, but it still matters now.

EXAMPLES
  • I've lost my keys.They are still lost right now.
  • It's been years since I last saw her.I haven't seen her for years, and that's still true today.
  • That's the third time I've called the coach.I'm counting the times up to now, and I may call again.
  • It's the first time I've been on a plane.It's is present, so the verb goes with have/has.
SIMPLE OR PERFECT?
SIMPLE — closed
I lost my keys yesterday.Yesterday is finished, so there's no link to now.
PERFECT — still open
I've lost my keys.Still lost as I speak.

✕C — had asked. This works only if the first part is past: It was the second time she had asked.

HOW LONG, UP TO NOW

Present perfect continuous

FORM
have / has + been + verb-ing
THE EXTRA IDEA

It started in the past and is still going. You're saying how long, not what's done.

EXAMPLES
  • I've been waiting outside since midnight.I'm still waiting, and how long matters.
  • She's been training for the competition all month.She started a month ago and is still training.
RESULT OR ACTIVITY?
PERFECT — the result
I've read three chapters.How many chapters — the result.
PERFECT CONTINUOUS — the activity
I've been reading all afternoon.How long I read — and maybe I haven't finished.
THE PAST BEFORE THE PAST

Past perfect

FORM
had + past participle
THE EXTRA IDEA

One past action happened before another past action.

EXAMPLES
  • I'd left my keys at the office when I got home.Leaving them came first; getting home came second.
  • By the time she arrived, the film had started.The film started before she got there.
  • It was the first time I'd seen snow.It was is past, so the verb goes one step further back.
Watch the 'd: here it is had — had left, had seen — because a past participle follows it.
WOULD OR HAD?

She'd finished the book before the film came out.

A'd = had
B'd = would
Tap to check
✓A — 'd = had. Yes. Finished is a past participle, so 'd must be had.
✕B — 'd = would. Would needs a base verb — she'd finish. The -ed form here rules it out.
HOW LONG, BEFORE A PAST POINT

Past perfect continuous

FORM
had + been + verb-ing
THE EXTRA IDEA

It went on for a while and then stopped before another past moment, and often explains it.

EXAMPLES
  • She was tired because she'd been dancing for hours.The dancing is why she was tired.
  • I'd been carrying that spare key for months.I carried it for months, up to that day.
DONE OR ONGOING?
PAST PERFECT — finished
She'd written the report.The report was finished.
PAST PERFECT CONTINUOUS — the activity
She'd been writing for hours.How long she wrote — maybe she didn't finish.
A SHORTCUT

Having done something

FORM
Having + past participle, …
THE EXTRA IDEA

It says after + had in one word. The first action is finished before the main one.

EXAMPLES
  • Having read it twice, she felt confident.= After she had read it twice, she felt confident.
  • Having trained all year, he was ready for the match.The training came first, and it explains why.
  • Having lived in Japan, she spoke fluent Japanese.Here it also gives the reason: because she had lived there.
MIND THIS
The subject must be the same on both sides.
Having read it, the report seemed clear.
✓ Having read it, she found the report clear.

THREE THAT LOOK ALIKE

Used to · be used to · get used to

USED TO + BASE VERB
A past habit that stopped.I used to dance every day.
BE USED TO + -ING
Already familiar with it.I'm used to dancing every day.
GET USED TO + -ING
Becoming familiar with it.I'm getting used to training early.
