THE BASELINE

Past simple

FORM
subject + verb-ed / irregular verb
EXAMPLES
  • I got home at eleven.It happened and it's over.
  • She trained every day last year.She doesn't do it anymore.
Every tense below is the past simple plus one extra idea. Compare them with this one.
IN PROGRESS

Past continuous

FORM
was / were + verb-ing
THE EXTRA IDEA

The action wasn't finished. It was happening at that moment in the past.

EXAMPLES
  • It was raining when I arrived.The rain started before I arrived and kept going.
  • I was studying at eight last night.I was in the middle of it at that time.
SIMPLE OR CONTINUOUS?
SIMPLE
When she called, I cooked dinner.First the call, then I cooked.
CONTINUOUS
When she called, I was cooking dinner.I was already in the middle of cooking.
CHOOSE ONE

I _____ my homework when the lights went out.

Awas doing
Bdid
Tap to check
✓A — was doing. Yes. I was in the middle of the homework when the lights went out.
✕B — did. This means I did the homework after the lights went out. You need the action that was already happening.
PAST TOUCHING NOW

Present perfect

FORM
have / has + past participle
THE EXTRA IDEA

It happened in the past, but it still matters now.

EXAMPLES
  • I've lost my keys.They are still lost right now.
  • It's been years since I last saw her.I haven't seen her for years, and that's still true today.
  • That's the third time I've called the coach.I'm counting the times up to now, and I may call again.
  • It's the first time I've been on a plane.It's is present, so the verb goes with have/has.
SIMPLE OR PERFECT?
SIMPLE — closed
I lost my keys yesterday.Yesterday is finished, so there's no link to now.
PERFECT — still open
I've lost my keys.Still lost as I speak.
ONE NEGATIVE PER SENTENCE
I haven't done nothing.
✓ I haven't done anything.
✓ I have done nothing.
English allows only one negative word per clause. Portuguese allows two.
CHOOSE ONE

It's the second time she _____ that question today.

Ahas asked
Basked
Chad asked
Tap to check
✓A — has asked. Yes. It's the second time is present, so the verb takes has.
✕B — asked. No. It's points to now, so the verb has to reach now too.
✕C — had asked. This works only if the first part is past: It was the second time she had asked.
TAP THE SENTENCE TO FIX IT
It's been years that I last saw her.
Tap to fix
It's been years since + past simple. Since marks the point you count from.
I was to Paris three times.
Tap to fix
No finished time is given, so you count up to now with have/has.
HOW LONG, UP TO NOW

Present perfect continuous

FORM
have / has + been + verb-ing
THE EXTRA IDEA

It started in the past and is still going. You're saying how long, not what's done.

EXAMPLES
  • I've been waiting outside since midnight.I'm still waiting, and how long matters.
  • She's been training for the competition all month.She started a month ago and is still training.
RESULT OR ACTIVITY?
PERFECT — the result
I've read three chapters.How many chapters — the result.
PERFECT CONTINUOUS — the activity
I've been reading all afternoon.How long I read — and maybe I haven't finished.
THE PAST BEFORE THE PAST

Past perfect

FORM
had + past participle
THE EXTRA IDEA

One past action happened before another past action.

EXAMPLES
  • I'd left my keys at the office when I got home.Leaving them came first; getting home came second.
  • By the time she arrived, the film had started.The film started before she got there.
  • It was the first time I'd seen snow.It was is past, so the verb goes one step further back.
Watch the 'd: here it is had — had left, had seen — because a past participle follows it.
WOULD OR HAD?

She'd finished the book before the film came out.

A'd = had
B'd = would
Tap to check
✓A — 'd = had. Yes. Finished is a past participle, so 'd must be had.
✕B — 'd = would. Would needs a base verb — she'd finish. The -ed form here rules it out.
HOW LONG, BEFORE A PAST POINT

Past perfect continuous

FORM
had + been + verb-ing
THE EXTRA IDEA

It went on for a while and then stopped before another past moment, and often explains it.

EXAMPLES
  • She was tired because she'd been dancing for hours.The dancing is why she was tired.
  • I'd been carrying that spare key for months.I carried it for months, up to that day.
DONE OR ONGOING?
PAST PERFECT — finished
She'd written the report.The report was finished.
PAST PERFECT CONTINUOUS — the activity
She'd been writing for hours.How long she wrote — maybe she didn't finish.
THE ONE THAT TRIPS EVERYONE

'd — would or had?

THE TEST

Look at the verb that comes next. Base verb → would. Past participle → had.

EXAMPLES
  • I'd love to come.love is a base verb, so 'd is would.
  • I'd left before she called.left is a participle, so 'd is had.
  • She'd been waiting for hours.been is a participle, so 'd is had.
THE ONE EXCEPTION

'd better is always had better — and the verb after better stays in the base form.I'd better leave now. = I had better leave now.

WOULD OR HAD?

I'd rather stay home tonight.

Awould
Bhad
Tap to check
✓A — would. Stay is a base verb, so 'd is would. I would rather stay home.
✗B — had. Had needs a participle after it. There is no had rather.
SAME WORDS, DIFFERENT TIME

I'd rather — present or past?

ABOUT ME

I'd rather + base verb.I'd rather stay home tonight.

ABOUT SOMEONE ELSE, NOW OR LATER

I'd rather + past simple. Past form, but it is about tonight.I'd rather you stayed home tonight.

ABOUT THE PAST

I'd rather + past perfect. It already happened, and you wish it hadn't.I'd rather you had stayed home last night.

CHOOSE ONE

I'd rather you _____ me before you left yesterday.

Atold
Bhad told
Tap to check
✓B — had told. Yesterday is over, so you need the past perfect. You are looking back at something that already happened.
✗A — told. That form is for now or later: I'd rather you told me tomorrow.
FIND THE MISTAKE
I'd rather you stay home tonight.
Tap to fix
When the preference is about someone else, the verb takes the past form — even for tonight.
I'd rather you didn't say that last week.
Tap to fix
Last week is finished, so the verb steps back one more level, to the past perfect.
PAST INTENTIONS

Plans that didn't happen

WAS GOING TO

A plan you had, and usually did not carry out.I was going to call you, but I forgot.

WAS ABOUT TO

On the very edge of doing it — seconds away.I was about to leave when she arrived.

WOULD LIKE TO HAVE

A wish that never came true.I'd like to have stayed longer.

CHOOSE ONE

I _____ open the door when the phone rang.

Awas going to
Bwas about to
Tap to check
✓B — was about to. The phone rang at that exact second, so you were right on the edge of opening it.
✗A — was going to. Not wrong, but weaker: it says you had a plan, not that you were one second away.
A SHORTCUT

Having done something

FORM
Having + past participle, …
THE EXTRA IDEA

It says after + had in one word. The first action is finished before the main one.

EXAMPLES
  • Having read it twice, she felt confident.= After she had read it twice, she felt confident.
  • Having trained all year, he was ready for the match.The training came first, and it explains why.
  • Having lived in Japan, she spoke fluent Japanese.Here it also gives the reason: because she had lived there.
MIND THIS
The subject must be the same on both sides.
Having read it, the report seemed clear.
✓ Having read it, she found the report clear.
TAP THE SENTENCE TO FIX IT
Having finish the exam, she went home.
Tap to fix
After having, the verb takes the past participle — never the base form.
Have lived abroad, he spoke three languages.
Tap to fix
The structure starts with having. Have lived would need a subject in front of it.
THREE THAT LOOK ALIKE

Used to · be used to · get used to

USED TO + BASE VERB
A past habit that stopped.I used to dance every day.
BE USED TO + -ING
Already familiar with it.I'm used to dancing every day.
GET USED TO + -ING
Becoming familiar with it.I'm getting used to training early.
CHOOSE ONE

It was hard at first, but now I'm used to _____ up at six.

Agetting
Bget
Tap to check
✓A — getting. Yes. After be used to, the verb takes -ing.
✕B — get. That's the pattern for plain used to, the past habit. After be used to, the verb needs -ing.
TAP THE SENTENCE TO FIX IT
I'm used to dance every day.
Tap to fix
After be used to and get used to, the verb takes -ing. Only plain used to takes the base verb.
I used to having long hair.
Tap to fix
Plain used to — the past habit — is followed by the base verb.
TAP TO CHECK

Irregular past participles

WHY THEY MATTER

Every perfect tense needs the participle, not the past simple. I've seen it — never I've saw it.
