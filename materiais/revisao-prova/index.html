GRAMMAR 1

Passive voice

WHEN WE USE IT — FROM YOUR SHEET

When we are more interested in the action than in the agent.

When we are more interested in the receiver of the action than in the agent.

When the agent is not important, not known, or obvious.

FORMATION
a form of be + past participle

The tense lives in be. The participle never changes.

The four forms on your sheet

VERB TENSE
PASSIVE VOICE
Simple present
am / is / are + past participleThe table is set every evening.
Present continuous
am / is / are being + past participleThe table is being set right now.
Simple past
was / were + past participleThe table was set an hour ago.
Modal verbspresent
can / must / should / could / may / might + be + past participleThe table must be set before seven.
Those four are what the test is built on. Learn them as four shapes, not as a rule: is set · is being set · was set · must be set.
THE FOUR MISTAKES THAT COST MARKS
The present given to the teacher.The present was given to the teacher.No be, no passive. The most common one.
The table was set by she.The table was set by her.After by, object pronoun: me, him, her, us, them.
The students was told to wait.The students were told to wait.be agrees with the new subject, not with the old one.
The accident was happened at six.The accident happened at six.happen, arrive, die, go, come, fall take no object, so they have no passive.

GRAMMAR 1 — PART TWO

The two notes under the chart

1. get instead of be

In informal, non-standard English, get sometimes replaces be in a passive. Your sheet marks it as non-standard, so recognise it — but write be in the test unless you are asked for the informal version.

  • Henry got promoted last week.= Henry was promoted. Informal.
  • Sandra got stood up this morning. She was waiting for the guy, but he never showed up.= Sandra was stood up. stand someone up = fail to meet them as arranged.
This is the bridge between the two halves of the unit: the same get that builds get dressed and get lost is the one that sneaks into passives.

2. Verbs with two objects

Some verbs take a direct object (the thing) and an indirect object (the person). Those verbs have two possible passives.

ACTIVE
The students gave a present to the teacher.
PASSIVE — THING FIRST
A present was given to the teacher (by the students).The thing becomes the subject, and the person keeps to.
PASSIVE — PERSON FIRST
The teacher was given a present (by the students).The person becomes the subject, and to disappears.

Step by step

Follow the six steps in order. Don't try to hear the right answer — build it.

Find the two objects
One is a person, one is a thing. They gave information to us.
Decide which one starts the sentence
Either works. Starting with the person is the more natural one in English.
If it's a pronoun, make it a subject pronoun
A sentence can't start with me, us or them. Change it: us → we. And drop the to — a subject never starts with to.
Look at the tense of the active verb, and put be in that tense
gave is simple past, so you need was or were. Which one depends on your new subject: we is plural, so were.
Change the main verb to the past participle
give → gave → given. Third column, never the second.
Write the rest of the sentence
Person first → the to is gone for good. Thing first → the person keeps its to.
WORKED EXAMPLE — STARTING WITH THE PERSON
They gave information to us.
1Two objects: information (thing) and us (person).
2I'll start with the person: us.
3us is an object pronoun → we. And the to goes in the bin.
4gave = simple past; we is plural → were.
5give → given.
6Add what's left: information.
We were given information.
SAME SENTENCE — STARTING WITH THE THING
They gave information to us.
2This time I start with the thing: information.
3It isn't a pronoun, so nothing to change — and us keeps its to, because it is not the subject.
4information is singular → was.
5give → given.
Information was given to us.
THE THREE WAYS THIS GOES WRONG
To us was given information.We were given information.Never start a passive with to. The moment the person becomes the subject, the to disappears.
Us were given information.We were given information.Object pronoun in subject position. Change it: us → we.
We were give information.We were given information.Second column instead of third. After be, always the participle.

GRAMMAR 2

Discourse markers — chart 1

A discourse marker adds no information. It tells the reader what kind of idea is coming next. Your sheet lists their jobs: showing turns, joining ideas, changing a topic, adding information, contrasting, ordering speech, reason and effect, concluding.

ADDITION
and · moreover · furthermore · in addition · also · besides · along with this
CAUSE, REASON OR EFFECT
because · for · as · since · therefore · given that · due to
COMPARISON
like · as · unlike · similarly · in the same way · just as · in comparison
CONSEQUENCE OR RESULT
consequently · as a result · so · thus · hence · in conclusion · because (of)
CONTRAST
but · however · even though · nevertheless · yet · whereas · on the contrary
Three words appear in more than one column. as is cause, comparison and condition. like is comparison and illustration. because is cause and because of is result. Don't memorise them by column — memorise what comes after them.
GRAMMAR 2 — PART TWO

Discourse markers — chart 2

EMPHASIS
in fact · as a matter of fact · indeed · actually · in reality · essentially · fortunately
These add weight to what you just said, or correct it. It wasn't expensive. In fact, it was free.
CONDITION
if · whether · as long as · provided that · assuming that · unless · now that
unless already means if not. unless you don't come is a double negative. Unless you come, I'll go alone.
ILLUSTRATING
for example · for instance · in some cases · such as · like · let's say · be it
ORDERING
first · second · then · next · now · continuing · finally
PURPOSE
so that · to · not to · in order to · in order not to · so as to · so as not to
Purpose answers why? with an aim, not with a cause. She left early in order to catch the bus.
SO, SO THAT, SO AS TO — THREE DIFFERENT THINGS

so = result. It was late, so we left.

so that = purpose, followed by a subject and a verb. We left early so that we wouldn't miss it.

so as to = purpose, followed by an infinitive. We left early so as to catch the bus.

GRAMMAR 2 — PART THREE

What comes after it

Your sheet gives you the meanings. This is where the marks actually go: students pick the right idea and then put the wrong kind of thing after the marker.

MARKER
WHAT FOLLOWS
because · since · as · given that
subject + verbWe left because it was late.
due to · because of
noun or -ingWe left because of the time.
even though · whereas
subject + verbEven though it rained, we went.
however · nevertheless · therefore · consequently · moreover
a new sentence, then a commaIt rained. However, we went.
but · yet · so · and
join two clauses with a comma beforeIt rained, but we went.
such as · like · unlike
nouncrops such as soya and corn
so that · provided that · as long as
subject + verbTake it, provided that you give it back.
in order to · so as to · to
infinitive without to after itShe called in order to explain.
DON'T DO THIS
It rained all day, however we still went.It rained all day. However, we still went.however cannot glue two clauses with a comma. Full stop or semicolon.
Even though it rained, but we went.Even though it rained, we went.One contrast word per contrast. Never both.
Due to it was late, we left.Because it was late, we left.due to needs a noun, not a clause.

VOCABULARY 1

Collocations with get and set

Your sheet gives eight. These eight are the test. Learn each one as one word, with its example attached — don't build them from get plus something.

GET
You become something.A state you weren't in before. Followed by an adjective or a participle.
SET
You fix something.You decide or arrange it on purpose. Followed by a noun.
THE EIGHT — TAP TO SEE THE MEANING
get dressed
Put your clothes on. It takes her half an hour to get dressed in the morning.
get drunk
Become drunk. Lisa got drunk last night and had to take a taxi home.
get lost
Lose your way. Without your help, I would get lost. Thank you!
get sick
Become ill. Gosh, this is the second time I've gotten sick this month!
set a precedent
Do something that becomes the model for what comes after. What you have done will set a legal precedent.
set an example
Behave in a way others should copy. Parents and teachers should set an example.
set the date and time
Decide when something will happen. Have you set the date and time for Jason's farewell party?
set the table
Put the plates and cutlery out before a meal. She hasn't set the table yet. How can I serve dinner?
TWO THINGS IN THE EXAMPLES WORTH NOTICING

gotten is the American past participle of get. British English says got. Your sheet uses the American one — both are correct.

a precedent is a first case that others then follow. A legal precedent is a court decision that later decisions copy.

The pattern underneath: after get comes an adjective or participle — dressed, drunk, lost, sick. After set comes a noun — a precedent, an example, the date, the table. If the gap is followed by a noun with a or the, it is almost always set.

The success family

Your sheet lists nine forms. The test will give you one and ask for another. Decide the class of word first, the meaning second.

THE QUESTION TO ASK, IN THIS ORDER

1. Is there a, the or her before the gap? → noun.

2. Is there a noun straight after, or was / is / felt / seemed before? → adjective.

3. Is the sentence already complete without it? → adverb, ending in -ly.

4. Only then ask whether it needs to be negative.

CHECK YOURSELF 4 · ONE OF THESE IS NOT WHAT YOU THINK
They lost three ___ matches, one after the other.
Asuccessful
Bsuccessive
Csuccessfully
Show the answer
Bsuccessive = one after another. The sentence says one after the other, and it is about losing.
Asuccessful would mean they won them — the opposite of what the sentence says.
Csuccessfully is an adverb. Before a noun you need an adjective.
THE ONE THAT WILL TRICK YOU — SUCCESSIVE

It sits in the family and it is not about succeeding.

successive = one after another, in a row. They lost three successive matches. — three defeats, no success anywhere.

If the sentence is about doing well, successive is the wrong answer. If it is about things in a sequence, it is the right one.

SPELLING

Two c, two s: success. And one l in successful, two in successfully.

The negative prefix goes on the front, the suffix stays on the back: un + successful + ly.

THE NIGHT BEFORE

Mixed test

Twelve gaps, all four topics mixed up, in no order — the way the real test will feel. Do it without scrolling back up.

12 QUESTIONS
Tap the option you think is right. You'll know straight away. If you get fewer than 9, go back to the section it came from.
1The form ___ before Friday.
After a modal: modal + be + past participle.
2Parents and teachers should ___.
set an example. An example is a noun you fix on purpose, and it keeps its article.
3It rained all morning. ___, the match went ahead.
The second idea goes against the first, so it is contrast. Therefore is result, because is cause.
4After two ___ attempts, she passed.
attempts is a noun, so the gap in front of it is an adjective.
5The report ___ right now, so please wait.
right now forces the continuous: is being + participle.
6Without a map I always ___ lost.
lost is a participle used like an adjective, and get is the verb that goes with those.
7The trip was cancelled ___ the weather.
the weather is a noun. due to takes a noun; because needs a subject and a verb.
8Marina ___ a present by the class.
Two objects, person first: Marina was given a present. gave would mean she did the giving.
9She studies at night ___ keep up with the class.
An infinitive follows, so you need in order to. so that would need a subject and a verb after it.
10Have you ___ the date and time yet?
set the date and time — you decide it. And set never changes its form.
11The project was ___ finished on time.
The gap describes finished, which is a verb, so you need the adverb.
12Soya ___ by local farmers.
by local farmers gives the agent, so the sentence is passive: is + grown, the third form.
