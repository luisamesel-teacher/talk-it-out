Gap-fill & word building

Two short exercises. Tap your way through them — no writing needed.

EXERCISE 1

One word in each gap

Type one word in each gap — never two. Most of them are small grammar words: prepositions, adverbs, pronouns.

Ten years ago, almost every teenager owned a CD player. Today that is no true: streaming has taken over. The demand physical albums fell so fast that hundreds of shops closed. this, vinyl came back. Collectors decided to give up on records, and sales rose as quickly as they had dropped. Some fans buy vinyl than streaming because they want something they can hold. If you already own , you know the feeling. Others collect records of pure nostalgia.

EXERCISE 2

Turning verbs and adjectives into nouns

Tap each line to see the noun. Read the group heading first — the ending tells you what kind of noun it makes.

-MENT
From a verb: the action, or its result.
fulfil→fulfilment
amend→amendment
detach→detachment
achieve→achievement
-ER · -OR
From a verb: the person or thing that does it.
seek→seeker
advise→adviser / advisor
compete→competitor
-ION · -ATION
From a verb: the process or the act.
decide→decision
determine→determination
transform→transformation
-NESS · -ITY
From an adjective: the quality itself.
aware→awareness
willing→willingness
able→ability
curious→curiosity
WATCH OUT
  • fulfil → fulfilment, with one L in British English.
  • decide → decision: the d becomes s.
  • able → ability and curious → curiosity: the word changes shape, not just the ending.
  • spite makes an adjective, not a noun: spiteful.
EXERCISE 3

Now try it yourself

Say the missing word out loud, then tap to check.

She finally found FULFIL in teaching.
fulfilment
His DETACH surprised everyone at the meeting.
detachment
The committee voted on the AMEND yesterday.
amendment
Her ABLE to stay calm impressed the judges.
ability
He made the comment SPITE, just to hurt her.
spitefully
