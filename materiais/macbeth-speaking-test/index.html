9th grade · Speaking · 15 points

Speaking Test Macbeth

Five minutes. A card you don't choose. A side you might not believe in. Prepare all of it.

The format

In pairs or groups of three. It lasts about five minutes and it is all in English. You need to have read the book and to be ready to give an opinion and defend it.

  • 1Interview

    Personal questions. Nothing about Macbeth.

  • 2Questions

    Your pair gets a question. Student A answers it and defends that answer; Student B takes the opposite side. Then a new question for B, with A arguing against.

  • 3Decide together

    One question for the two of you. Argue it out and agree on a single answer.

Based on the Cambridge B2 First (FCE) Speaking paper, with Cambridge's own marking criteria. Part 1 is their Interview, Part 2 their Long turn, Part 3 their Collaborative task.

The clock

Use the time you are given. If you pass the time, you will be interrupted. On the other hand, stopping too early makes you lose marks.

  • Name and age, for the recording10s
  • Part 1 — personal questions~1 min
  • Part 2 — reading each question15s
  • Part 2 — answering and defending45s
  • Part 2 — arguing against your partner25–30s
  • Part 3 — the question, discussed together80–90s
Groups of three: one question each, and the student who argues against rotates. Your Part 3 question has three options — one each to start, then agree on one.

Everything else

What is expected

What earns marks in each part — and the language that earns them.

  • 1Part 1

    Never a one-word answer. Always a reason or an example.

  • 2Part 2 — answering

    Answer the question clearly, give two reasons, and use something that happens in the book as evidence. You do not have to believe it, either side can get full marks.

  • 2Part 2 — arguing against

    Take the opposite side, politely. One good counter-argument, not three weak ones.

  • 3Part 3

    Talk to each other. Ninety seconds is room for about six turns (two or three arguments from each of you) and then the final decision. Consider more than one option before you decide. Two separate opinions with no agreement does not complete the task.

Gains marks

Using the useful language, disagreeing politely, correcting yourself, asking your partner what they think, naming something from the book.

Loses marks

Reciting a memorised text, one-word answers, letting your partner do the work, speaking Portuguese, reading from a paper. The book stays closed.

Useful language

This is what you are marked on. Learn a few from each box and use them.

Part 1

Extending your answer

Yes, definitely — in fact, I'd go as far as to say…/Not really, no. The thing is…/It depends, actually. If…, then…/I suppose so, although I've never really thought about it.

Part 2 — answering

Taking a position

I'd argue that…/To a large extent, yes — though not entirely./There's a strong case for saying…/I'm inclined to think…

Conceding, then pushing back

Admittedly…, but that doesn't change the fact that…/While it's true that…, I'd still argue…/Even if…, the point remains that…

Using the book as evidence

This comes across most clearly when…/You only have to look at the banquet scene…/What really stands out is…/That's exactly what happens when…

Speculating

He might well have…/She must have known…/It's quite possible that…/By then, I'd imagine…

Part 2 — arguing against

Disagreeing politely — never “You're wrong”

I see your point, but I'd put it differently./That's true up to a point, although…/I take your point, and yet…/I'm not entirely convinced, because…/That's one way of looking at it, but…

Part 3

Bringing your partner in

What do you make of that?/Where do you stand on this?/Would you go along with that?

Partly agreeing

I'd go along with that, up to a point./There's something in that, although…/Fair enough — but only if…

Reaching the decision — you must finish with one

So, shall we go with…?/Let's settle on…/Can we agree on that?/Right, so our final answer is…/Why don't we meet in the middle?

Any part

Can't find the word

It's a kind of…/I don't know the word, but it's when you…

Need a second

That's a tricky one./Let me think for a second./How can I put this…

Correcting yourself — gains marks

Sorry, what I meant was…/Let me rephrase that./Or rather…

Didn't understand

Sorry, could you repeat that?/Do you mean…?

Who's who

Moments from the book

Evidence you can use in Part 2. Each one can be read more than one way.

Lady Macbeth reads the letter p. 24

“You are ambitious, but not bold enough / To do what must be done.”

Is she pushing him, or saying out loud what he already wants?

Macbeth hesitates p. 29

“King Duncan trusts me… I am his host. / I should protect him, keep him from all harm. … Ambition is my only spur and that may be my downfall.”

He lists every reason not to do it — and does it anyway.

She calls him a coward p. 30

“Are you a coward after all, afraid to act? / I cannot and I will not love a coward.”

That word is what changes his mind.

The point of no return p. 60

“I'm in so far that going back / Would be as difficult as going on.”

A man who cannot stop, or one who has chosen not to?

Lady Macbeth sleepwalking pp. 77–79

“The blood's still on my hands. I can smell it! … What's done cannot be undone.”

She said a little water would clean their hands.

Also worth thinking about

From Points for Understanding, no single right answer:

  1. Did Lady Macbeth really faint, or was she acting? (Act 2, q. 6)
  2. Would you put a real actor on stage as Banquo's ghost? (Act 3, q. 5)

Part 1 questions

Chosen at random on the day, from both lists. Nothing to study here — these are about you. Just never answer in one word.

Everyday questions

  1. Where do you live? What do you like about it?
  2. Tell me about your family.
  3. What do you usually do at the weekend?
  4. What's your favourite subject at school? Why?
  5. Do you play any sport? How often?
  6. What kind of music do you listen to?
  7. Tell me about a film or series you enjoyed recently.
  8. What did you do on your last holiday?
  9. Do you spend much time on your phone? Doing what?
  10. Is there a job you'd like to do in the future?

Questions connected to the play

  1. Do you usually make decisions quickly, or do you take your time?
  2. Who do you ask for advice when you have a difficult decision?
  3. Have you ever done something just because your friends were doing it?
  4. Have you ever changed your mind about something important?
  5. What's the bravest thing you've ever done?
  6. Are you superstitious about anything?
  7. Are you good at keeping secrets?
  8. Have you ever regretted something you said?

Part 2 questions

Two of these per pair, at random. Prepare all eleven: for each, find one thing in the book that supports a yes and one that supports a no. You will not know which side you get.

Guilt and responsibility

  1. Is Lady Macbeth more guilty than her husband?
  2. Was killing Banquo worse than killing Duncan?

Fate and the witches

  1. Can the witches be trusted?

Courage and fear

  1. Was Macbeth a brave man?
  2. At the end, does Macbeth kill out of fear or out of ambition?
  3. Could Macbeth have stopped after killing Duncan?

Women and power

  1. Would Lady Macbeth be judged the same way if she were a man?
  2. Is Lady Macbeth the strongest character in the play?

Kings and leaders

  1. Should Macduff be the king after killing Macbeth?

Choices and consequences

  1. Was Macduff right to leave his family in Scotland?
  2. Was telling his wife about the prophecy Macbeth's real mistake?

Part 3 questions

Agree on a single answer — not two separate opinions.

  1. Who is most responsible for Duncan's death — Macbeth, Lady Macbeth, or the witches? Choose one.
  2. Who suffers the most in this play? Choose one person.
  3. Which is more dangerous: ambition or fear?
  4. Lady Macbeth is on trial. Guilty or not guilty? Agree on one verdict.
  5. Which moment changes everything in the play? Agree on one scene.
  6. If Macbeth had never met the witches, would he still have become king?
  7. Your school is staging Macbeth. Which single scene must be included?
  8. Is Macbeth a villain or a victim? You must choose one word.
  9. What is the most important lesson of this play? Agree on one.
  10. Who is the most intelligent character in the play? Choose one.

Groups of three

Three options, one each to start, then agree on one.

  1. Who is most responsible for Duncan's death — Macbeth, Lady Macbeth, or the witches?
  2. What does the most damage in this play — ambition, fear, or pride?
  3. Who is the most tragic character — Macbeth, Lady Macbeth, or Lady Macduff?
  4. Which murder costs Macbeth the most — Duncan, Banquo, or Macduff's family?
  5. Who could have stopped all of this — Macbeth, Lady Macbeth, or Banquo?
  6. Who is the most intelligent character — Lady Macbeth, Macduff, or the witches?

How it's marked

15 points in total. Five criteria, 3 points each, marked individually. Half points possible — the bands between.
Band Grammar and Vocabulary Discourse Management Pronunciation Interactive Communication Content and evidence
3 Accurate use of the grammar and vocabulary studied. Ideas organised and developed; little hesitation. Easy to understand; good word stress. Starts, responds, disagrees, helps reach the decision. Argument based on what happens in the book.
2.5 Performance shares features of Bands 2 and 3.
2 Some slips, meaning clear. Developed with effort; some repetition. Clear most of the time. Takes part, but mostly reacts — or does all the talking. General, little evidence.
1.5 Performance shares features of Bands 1 and 2.
1 Limited; mistakes get in the way. Very short answers; loses the thread. Hard to follow. Needs the partner to keep the conversation going. No sign of having read it.
0 Nothing to assess, or the task was not attempted.
The three things that lift your mark most: add a reason or an example every time; paraphrase instead of stopping; disagree politely at least once in Part 3.

How to prepare

Four things, in this order.

  1. For each of the 11 Part 2 questions, find one thing in the book that supports a yes and one that supports a no.
  2. Decide what you think about the Part 3 questions — and be ready to change your mind.
  3. Learn five expressions from the useful language.
  4. Practise out loud with a timer. Forty-five seconds is the hard part.
You will not know which question you get, which side you have to take, or who your partner is. The only preparation that works is preparing all of them.

Practise here

A full run, timed like the real thing. Questions are drawn at random — speak out loud until the clock stops.

Ready?

You will get one Part 1 question from each list, one Part 2 question, and one Part 3 question. Nothing is saved — run it as many times as you like.
