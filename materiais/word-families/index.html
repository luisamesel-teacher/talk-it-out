THE RULE OF THIS PAGE

Cover, then say

ALWAYS IN THIS ORDER

verb → noun → person → adjective → adverb → negative

The order is the path. Say it the same way every time and it becomes automatic.

IN A TEST, TWO STEPS

1. What class does the gap need? Noun, adjective or adverb?

2. Then the word.

Choosing the word first is what goes wrong. Every time.

Three new families a week. The old ones come back first, from memory, before the new ones go in.
THIS WEEK

Three families

These three come from your own work. Two of them caught you out already.

EDUCATE
verbeducate
nouneducation
personeducator
adjectiveeducational
adverbeducationally
negativeuneducated
educative exists, but almost nobody says it. The ordinary adjective is educational. Both are built correctly — only one is the real word.
OBJECT
verbobject (to something)
nounobjection — usually plural: objections
personobjector
adjectiveobjectionable
negativeunobjectionable
You raise objections, in the plural, far more often than one objection. When the sentence means "all the things she said against it", it is plural.
IMAGINE
verbimagine
nounimagination
adjectiveimaginative — full of new ideas
adjectiveimaginary — not real
adjectiveimaginable — possible to picture
adverbimaginatively
negativeunimaginative
Three adjectives, three meanings. An imaginative child invents games. An imaginary friend does not exist. The worst thing imaginable is the worst you can picture.

a / the / her before the gap → noun

a noun right after, or was / is / felt / seemed before → adjective

sentence already complete without it → adverb

THE PAIRS THAT TRICK YOU

Two real words, one right answer

No rule will save you here. These are the ones worth knowing by heart.

TAP TO SEE THE DIFFERENCE
economic · economical
economic = about the economy. economical = cheap to run. an economic crisis · an economical little car
historic · historical
historic = important enough to be remembered. historical = belonging to history. a historic victory · historical documents
imaginative · imaginary
imaginative = full of new ideas. imaginary = not real. an imaginative answer · an imaginary friend
sensitive · sensible
sensitive = feels things deeply, or is easily hurt. sensible = has good judgement. sensitive skin · a sensible decision
effective · efficient
effective = it works. efficient = it works without waste. an effective treatment · an efficient engine
continual · continuous
continual = again and again, with breaks. continuous = without stopping. continual interruptions · continuous rain
classic · classical
classic = the best example of its kind. classical = of ancient Greece and Rome, or the music. a classic mistake · classical music
industrial · industrious
industrial = about industry. industrious = hard-working. an industrial estate · an industrious student

PREFIXES

Two different machines

Some prefixes say no. Others change the meaning entirely. Mixing them up is why undergone looks like a negative and isn't.

MACHINE 1 — THEY SAY NO
un- · in- · im- · il- · ir- · dis- · non-
unhappy · incorrect · impossible · illegal · irregular · dishonest · non-stop
WHICH NEGATIVE? THE SPELLING DECIDES

im- before p, b, m — impossible, imbalance, immature

il- before l — illegal, illogical

ir- before r — irregular, irresponsible

in- for most of the rest — incorrect, inability

un- is the default, and the only one that works on participles — unfinished, unknown

MACHINE 2 — THEY CHANGE THE MEANING
over- · under- · re- · mis- · out- · pre- · co-
PREFIX
MEANING
over-
too muchoverconfident, overcooked, overworked
under-
too little — or to go throughunderpaid, underestimate · undergo a change
re-
againrewrite, rebuild, reconsider
mis-
wronglymisunderstand, misspell, misjudge
out-
more or better thanoutnumber, outrun, outperform
pre-
beforeprepaid, preview, prehistoric
co-
togethercooperate, co-author, coexist
undergo → underwent → undergone means to go through something, usually something you did not choose. The building has undergone major repairs. Nothing negative about it.
WHICH PREFIX?
One prefix per gap. Some say no, some change the meaning.
